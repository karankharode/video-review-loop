Solid-state batteries have been two years away for ten years.

Everyone assumed the holdup was chemistry.

It's not chemistry. It's pressure.

Inside one, lithium has to cross a ceramic wall.

That ceramic is harder than the lithium touching it.

So the lithium should lose.

It doesn't.

Researchers froze one mid-failure and looked.

The lithium finds a crack already there, fills it, and can't escape.

Pressure builds. The ceramic splits from the inside.

Soft metal splitting hard stone. Like a waterjet through rock.

That's the short circuit.

Not new chemistry. Better engineering.

Tougher ceramic. Coatings. And tiny deliberate voids that steer the crack somewhere harmless.

The problem was never the recipe. It was the pressure.
