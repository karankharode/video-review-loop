## LinkedIn

> "Just calm down" is the worst advice you can give someone before they speak.
>
> Calm is a long way from panic — physiologically, it's a big trip. Harvard research found a shorter one: reappraise the nerves as excitement. Say "I am excited" out loud. Same racing heart, different story about why. It improved performance across singing, public speaking and maths.
>
> The other half is that your nerves are far less visible than they feel. Speakers who were simply told that gave better speeches — and the improvement showed up in observers' ratings, not just their own.
>
> If you brief people before presentations, those two facts are worth more than an hour of slide feedback.

## Posting notes

- **The hook is the first frame.** No intro card, no logo sting.
- **Sound on.** The VO carries the argument; captions reinforce it.
- **Best slot** (Indian students): weekday evenings, 7–10pm IST. Also strong the
  night before a college fest, presentation week, or placement season.
- **Pin a first comment** with both source links. Sources in the comment reads as
  confident; sources in the caption reads as defensive.

## Do not claim

- Don't say this cures anxiety or social phobia. It is a reappraisal technique
  with measured effects on performance, not a treatment.
- Don't quote a prevalence stat. "75% of people fear public speaking" is
  everywhere and poorly sourced — it is deliberately not in this script.
- Don't say SAI teaches grammar, or promise fluency in N days.
- Sayable about SAI: voice practice, real-time feedback on spoken answers,
  interview scoring.
