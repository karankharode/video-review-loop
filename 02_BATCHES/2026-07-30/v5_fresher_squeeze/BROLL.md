# v5 — The Fresher Squeeze — B-ROLL

Six cutaways. Total b-roll coverage ≈ 22s of a 60s video — the rest is Karan on camera.

**This file is authoritative on cutaway timing.** `SCRIPT.md`'s shot table has been conformed to the coverage map at the bottom of this page, not the other way round. If the two ever disagree again, this file wins.

**Timings updated after the length pass.** The script came down from ~172 to 130 spoken words to be deliverable in 60s at an unhurried 145 wpm; every cutaway below has been re-timed to the line it actually covers. Every shot below is either phone-shootable in an afternoon or generatable as a motion-graphic in CapCut/After Effects. Nothing here needs a location permit, an actor's face, or a studio.

**Global rule:** all cutaways are *under* Karan's voice, not instead of it. He never stops talking. No b-roll shot runs longer than 4 seconds without cutting back to his face — the video's credibility is his face, and losing it for too long turns this into a generic news reel.

---

## B1 — The dead application list
**Covers VO:** "You've applied everywhere. You've started thinking it's you." (0–6.0s — used as a 1.5s flash-cut at 3.0s, *not* a full cutaway; the hook must stay on Karan, and "It's not." must land on his face at 4.5–6.0)
**Duration:** 1.5s
**On screen:** Laptop screen, filmed over the shoulder at a slight angle so it reads as real and not a screenshot. A job-portal application list — 8–10 rows, every status column reading "Applied" or "Application viewed". Cursor scrolls slowly down. No "Rejected" — the silence is the point, not rejection.
**How to get it:** Naukri or LinkedIn "My Jobs" page on your own account, or a mocked-up HTML page. Blur/redact company names. Shoot at 60fps, slow to 50% for the scroll.
**Note:** must be a *long* list. Three rows doesn't read as exhaustion.

## B2 — The counter
**Covers VO:** "FY22, Indian IT hired around six lakh freshers. FY25 — about one lakh twenty thousand. Eighty percent, gone."
**Duration:** 6.0s (6.0–12.0s), cut back to Karan at 12.0s so "Eighty percent, gone." lands on his face
**On screen:** Full-frame motion graphic. Large numeral `6,00,000` in the brand type, held for 1s, then a fast odometer roll down to `1,20,000` over ~2.5s, then it stops hard and a thin rule strikes through with `−80%` appearing beneath. Background: a heavily blurred, slightly desaturated plate of an office building exterior or a glass window with reflections, shot handheld so there's a little life in it.
**How to get it:** Numbers generated in CapCut (number counter effect) or AE. Background plate: any tech-park or glass-office exterior shot on a phone, defocused by shooting through a window or blurred in post at ~40px gaussian.
**Note:** use Indian numbering with commas (`6,00,000`), not `600,000`. It matters to this audience.
**Do not:** animate it bouncy or elastic. It should fall and stop.

## B3 — The joining date that moves
**Covers VO:** "One Wipro recruit's joining date was pushed seven months."
**Duration:** 4.0s (13.2–17.1s)
**On screen:** Phone held in hand, portrait, screen showing a generic offer-letter PDF scrolled to the "Date of Joining" field. Thumb scrolls. Then hard cut to a phone calendar app, month view, being swiped forward — August, September, October, November, December, January, February — seven swipes, accelerating. Ends on an empty month with nothing on it.
**How to get it:** Write a plain offer-letter PDF yourself with **no company name, no logo, no letterhead** — a generic template. Never show or imply a real Wipro document. Calendar swipes: your own phone calendar with events cleared.
**Legal note:** the VO names Wipro (sourced, factual, reported by Business Today). The *visual* must stay generic. Do not put a Wipro logo, letterhead, or lookalike branding on screen.

## B4 — The corridor of empty chairs
**Covers VO:** "But something did change. I won't pretend it didn't." — and only that. The cutaway ends before "Fewer roles. Same number of you.", which must land on Karan.
**Duration:** 4.0s (32.7–36.7s), cut back to Karan at 36.7s
**On screen:** A row of chairs against a corridor wall — the universal interview-waiting-room image. Slow handheld walk-past, or a slow push in toward a single closed door at the end of the corridor. All chairs empty. Cold, quiet, no people.
**How to get it:** Any college corridor, coworking space, clinic waiting area, or office lobby outside working hours. Shoot at golden hour or under fluorescents — both work, just commit to one. Handheld with a slight wobble reads better than gimbal-smooth here.
**Alternative if unavailable:** a single closed office door with a slow push-in, 4s. Simpler and nearly as good.
**Do not:** shoot people's faces. No recognisable individuals.

## B5 — Three questions on paper
**Covers VO:** "So pick three questions you know are coming."
**Duration:** 3.0s (49.0–52.0s)
**On screen:** Top-down, hands only, writing three lines in a notebook with a pen. The lines are legible: `1. Tell me about yourself` / `2. Why this company?` / `3. Walk me through a project`. Camera locked off, natural desk light.
**How to get it:** Phone on a stack of books or a cheap overhead arm. Shoot the writing in real time, speed up 1.5x in the edit. Write it neatly — this frame will get paused and screenshotted, which is the whole point of it existing.
**Why it's here:** this is the save-bait frame. It's the one image someone screenshots. Make it readable at thumbnail size.

---

## B6 — The voice memo
**Covers VO:** "Say them out loud. Record it. Listen back."
**Duration:** 3.6s (52.0–55.6s), cut back to Karan at 55.6s for the final eight words and the dead hold
**On screen:** Phone flat on the desk beside the notebook from B5, voice-memo app open and recording — waveform actively moving, timer counting up past 00:40. Then a thumb enters frame and taps play. The waveform starts moving again from the beginning.
**How to get it:** Your own phone, Voice Memos / Recorder app, record yourself actually talking so the waveform has real variation. Shoot from a low three-quarter angle so the screen is readable but you can still see the desk.
**Optional swap:** the SAI app's own practice-and-feedback screen instead of a generic voice memo — a real screen recording of a spoken answer being scored. **Only use this if the screen recording is of the actual live product.** If it isn't shipped and recordable today, shoot the generic voice memo — a fake product shot is worse than no product shot.
