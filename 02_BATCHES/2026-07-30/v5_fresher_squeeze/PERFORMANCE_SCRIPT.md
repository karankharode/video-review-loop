# v5 — The Fresher Squeeze — PERFORMANCE SCRIPT

**For Karan, on camera. Not to be read to TTS.**

Overall note before you start: this video is being watched by someone who is currently not getting called back. Do not perform sympathy at them. Perform *certainty* — you are telling them a fact about the market that nobody in their life has told them straight. The warmth comes from being blunt on their behalf, not from softening your voice.

Do not smile in the first 20 seconds. Smiling here reads as "content creator". You're not doing a bit.

Run it twice: once at your natural pace, once about 15% slower. The slower take is almost always the one.

**On length.** The script is 130 words. At 145 wpm that is 53.8s of speech; the four load-bearing pauses take about 3 seconds and the dead hold at the end takes one, which lands the piece at ~57.8s in a 60s slot. It was cut down from about 172 words specifically so you would never have to rush a line to make it fit. If a take comes in at 55s, that is fine and you should not fill the gap. If a take comes in over 60s, the pauses are not the problem — check whether you added words.

| 1 | "You've applied everywhere. You've started thinking it's you." | Start talking *before* the camera settles. No breath, no reset, no "okay so". Flat, matter-of-fact, like you're finishing a thought from a conversation that started off-camera. Land straight down the lens. |
| 2 | "It's not." | **Pause a full beat before this.** Two words, lower pitch than everything around them. This is the promise of the video. Do not rush past it. Then go straight into the number — there is no line announcing it any more, the counter graphic cuts in on the next frame. |
| 3 | "FY22, Indian IT hired around six lakh freshers. FY25 — about one lakh twenty thousand." | Cleanly, like a person quoting a figure they actually read, not reciting. Small pause on the dash before "about one lakh twenty thousand" — let the drop land as a drop. |
| 4 | "Eighty percent, gone." | Slow. Two chunks. Comma is real — take it. |
| 5 | "One Wipro recruit's joining date was pushed seven months." | Deadpan. Do not editorialise. The number is the reaction; you don't have to supply one. A tiny disbelieving headshake on "seven months" is allowed. One. |
| 6 | "When it dies in silence, that's not a verdict." | **Stop moving.** Hands down. This is the reset. Slow the whole line down, and take a real pause before it. If you've been gesturing, kill it here — stillness is what makes this read as true instead of as encouragement. ("It" is the application; the previous beat and the on-screen card have already established that, so don't re-explain it.) |
| 7 | "That's a smaller bench." | Almost thrown away. Cold, factual, no emphasis. The coldness is the point — it makes it structural rather than personal. |
| 8 | "It's a market condition. Not a character flaw." | **★ THIS IS THE LINE THAT CARRIES THE WHOLE VIDEO.** Slowest delivery in the entire script. Full stop between the two sentences — a real pause, not a comma. Look directly down the barrel. Do not blink through it. If you get one take right today, get this one. |
| 9 | "Nobody inside that process will say that to you, so I will." | Slight edge. A little annoyed on their behalf. This is the only opinionated line — let the opinion show. |
| 10 | "But something did change. I won't pretend it didn't." | **Look away on "But something did change" — off to your left, mid-distance — then come back to lens on "I won't pretend it didn't."** That look-away is what stops the video reading as a pep talk. It buys the honesty of the next 20 seconds. The full stop between the two sentences gives you a cleaner place to make the move than the old comma did. |
| 11 | "Fewer roles. Same number of you." | Clipped. Two short blocks. No warmth. This lands on your face, not on b-roll — the corridor cutaway ends before it. |
| 12 | "So the filter moved. Into one conversation you don't get to redo." | Lean in slightly on "into one conversation". Drop volume — not a whisper, just quieter. Quiet is more credible than loud here. Then let "you don't get to redo" sit for half a second at the end. |
| 13 | "You can't control their headcount." | Back to full voice. Slight shrug — genuinely, you can't, and that's fine. |
| 14 | "You can control that conversation." | Pick the pace up. This is the pivot into agency. Land "that conversation" hard. |
| 15 | "So pick three questions you know are coming." | Count it on your fingers if it's natural for you — three fingers up. If it feels staged, don't. |
| 16 | "Say them out loud. Record it. Listen back." | Three separate instructions, three separate beats. Small pause between each. Do not run them together — the viewer needs to be able to remember all three. |
| 17 | "It was never you —" | Callback to the hook. Softer than everything before it. This is the only line in the video where you're allowed to sound kind. |
| 18 | "— this part is yours." | Land it and stop. Do not add anything. Do not say "so yeah" or "alright" or gesture to a subscribe button. Hold the frame still for one second after you finish, eyes still on lens, then cut. The dead second is the ending. |

## Things to actively avoid in performance

- Any rising "creator" inflection at line ends. Statements, not questions.
- Filling the pauses. The pauses at lines 2, 6, 8 and 18 are load-bearing — and they are budgeted into the 60 seconds, so taking them costs you nothing. Silence will feel much longer to you than it does on playback.
- Sounding motivational on line 17–18. The video earns its ending by having been blunt for 55 seconds. Turning warm and loud at the end undoes it.
- Reading. If you catch yourself reciting, put the script down, take the beat in your own words, then re-tighten to the written line on the next take. The written words are correct — the recitation is what breaks.

## If a line won't land

Line 8 ("It's a market condition. Not a character flaw.") is the only one that must be word-perfect. Everywhere else, if a phrase fights your mouth, change it to how you'd actually say it — the meaning is what's fixed, not the syllables. Two constraints that don't move: never imply the viewer's job prospects depend on how well they speak, and never say anything that could be heard as blaming their English.
