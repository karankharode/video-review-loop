# B-roll generation brief — v4 "What Are You Practising For?"

**Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 30fps. Faceless throughout — no recognisable human face in any shot. Silhouettes, hands, backs of heads and stylised avatar figures only.

**Shots 1–6, 8 and 9 are generative. Shot 7 is a phone shoot** — see that section. It is the only shot in the video where a generator cannot be trusted, because it needs the same person on both sides of the frame.

---

## The split-screen system (read before generating anything)

Every shot from 0s to 39.8s uses the **same** vertical split, and the consistency is the whole point — the divider is the spine that makes the argument readable.

**Shot lengths are no longer a uniform 5.0s grid.** They are derived from their own VO lines (see the runtime check in `SCRIPT.md`), because the 22% line grew to carry its qualifier and the 25s line shrank. The retention beat still lands on 0:25.0.

- **Divider:** a 6px vertical line at exact centre, warm amber (#F4A93C), full height, never moves, never animates until 40s. It is the one element present in every frame of the first eight shots.
- **LEFT panel = THE SUPPLY.** What is available to you. Job listings, entry-level seats, the doorway. Cool palette — desaturated slate blue (#2B3A4A base), cold key light from screen-left.
- **RIGHT panel = THE SCREEN.** What you are judged on. Interview room, voice, waveform, feedback. Warm palette — deep charcoal with amber key light from screen-right.
- **What changes between sides across the video:** attention. Left is lit and active for 0–15s (the supply story), right is dark. At 15s the lighting hands over — right becomes lit and active, left dims to grey but never disappears. At 25s both are lit simultaneously for the first time, and that simultaneity *is* the retention beat. At 39.8s the divider dissolves and the two halves become one frame.
- **Continuity anchors that must recur:** (1) the empty interview chair with a table mic — appears at 0s on the right, returns at 40s occupied; (2) the amber divider colour, which becomes the countdown numeral colour, which becomes the save icon colour; (3) the waveform, flat in shot 7 and alive in shot 7's right half onward.
- **Camera:** every shot is a slow push-in of no more than 4%. No whip pans, no shake. The energy comes from the cuts and the captions, not the camera.

## Shot 7 — 0:29.4–0:34.6 (5.2s) — **SHOOT THIS, DO NOT GENERATE IT**

**VO:** "Three. Say your answers out loud. Reading them silently is not practice."

**This shot is a phone shoot, not a generative ask.** Both halves of the split must be the same real person from the same take. A generator will not reliably hold one identity across two halves of a frame, and the failure is invisible until it isn't — which turns this shot into an open-ended regeneration loop that eats the session for one 5-second beat.

**How to shoot it (about fifteen minutes):**
1. One person, one room, one outfit. Phone locked off on a stack of books or a cheap tripod. Do not touch the camera between takes.
2. Frame them **from behind, over the shoulder**, notebook and desk visible, face never in frame. This keeps the piece faceless and removes the identity problem entirely.
3. **Take A (goes on the left):** they read from the notebook in silence. Still shoulders, no lip movement. 10 seconds.
4. **Take B (goes on the right):** same seat, same posture start, same framing — now they answer the question out loud, with normal head and hand movement. 10 seconds.
5. Both halves are cropped from the same locked-off frame in the edit. Because the camera never moved, the room, desk, light and clothing match automatically.

**Grade in post, not on set:** cool the left half and warm the right, and add the flat vs alive waveform as an overlay on each side. Do not relight between takes — the temperature difference is a post decision so the two halves stay physically identical.

**Fallback if no second person is available:** shoot yourself from behind. The shot has no face in it, so it costs nothing.
