## The one thing the viewer leaves with

Octopuses have a snapped-in-two ribosomal RNA strand — broken at the exact spot the ribosome uses to check it's grabbing the right amino acid — and that break makes their protein-building measurably more accurate, though nobody has yet proven it's the reason octopuses are smart.

## Hook

**Spoken (13 words):** "Every octopus has a broken ribosome. The break is what makes it better."
**On-screen (3 words):** `BROKEN ON PURPOSE`

*(Reconciled: an earlier draft of this section read "The break makes it better" while the shot table and avatar script read "The break is what makes it better." The shot-table version is canonical and is now used in all four files.)*

**Alternates considered:**
- *"Octopus intelligence may not start in the brain at all."* — contradicts the right belief, but it's a promise with no picture attached. A viewer can't see it in 3 seconds.
- *"Their DNA is perfectly intact. The damage happens afterwards."* — factually the sharpest line in the paper (the rDNA locus is contiguous; the break is post-transcriptional), but it needs two sentences of setup before it means anything. Wrong job for second zero.

**Why the chosen one:** "Broken" carries an automatic assumption — broken machinery works worse. The second clause snaps that assumption in the same breath, so the contradiction is complete inside four seconds and the rest of the video is spent earning it rather than setting it up. It also states the subject (octopus) and the object (ribosome) immediately, which is what makes it survive a muted scroll with the on-screen type doing the work.

## Caption

An octopus's ribosome — the machine that builds every protein in its body — is physically snapped in two, right at the point that checks each amino acid is the correct one. The DNA is intact; the break happens after transcription. In vitro, octopus ribosomes made fewer errors than squid, sea slug and human ribosomes in the same test, and when researchers engineered that same break into E. coli, its error rate halved. Every shallow-water octopus they examined has it. The single deep-sea octopus they were able to check — one *Grimpoteuthis*, collected at 406 m — did not.

The link to intelligence is a hypothesis, not a result — the authors say so themselves.

Mitra, Han, Scott et al., Current Biology, 29 July 2026.

## Fact check

| Claim (as spoken) | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Octopuses have a break in their core 28S ribosomal RNA | Current Biology 2026 / preprint Fig. 1B–D: break localised between nucleotides G4322/C4323 in helix 88, confirmed by RT-PCR, RACE-PCR and 3.2 Å cryo-EM | High — direct result |
| It is unique among animals tested | Preprint Fig. 1D: RACE-PCR across phylogenetically diverse species (squid, cuttlefish, *Doryteuthis*, *Aplysia*, earthworm, human cells) found it only in octopus | High — direct result, but "tested" is the operative word; caption and VO say "octopuses have it", not "no other animal on Earth has it" |
| The break sits where the ribosome checks the correct amino acid | Preprint: H88 is in the E-site; DMS probing shows increased flexibility in A-site helices H89–93, the aa-tRNA accommodation corridor used for tRNA selection. Octopus ribosomes showed a 4-fold reduction in affinity for near-cognate (mismatched) tRNA pairing vs squid | High — but note the break is in the E-site and acts on the decoding A-site allosterically. The VO's plain-language phrasing is a compression, not a location claim |
| "In the dish, it makes fewer mistakes" | Preprint Fig. 2G: luciferase misincorporation reporter; octopus highest fidelity vs squid, *Aplysia*, human (n=5) | High — direct result, **in vitro**, and the VO now says so. "In the dish" is load-bearing and must not be trimmed for pace. |
| The same break in E. coli halves its errors | Preprint Fig. 2H–I: chimeric *E. coli* ribosomes carrying the octopus H88 break showed a 2-fold increase in fidelity, no change in translation efficiency (n=6). Science's coverage renders this as "about 50% fewer errors" | High — direct result |
| "The one deep-sea octopus they checked didn't have it" | Preprint Fig. 5D: a Cirrate (*Grimpoteuthis*), collected at 406 m by ROV, showed complete absence of the H88 break. Present in every Incirrate examined. Cirrates diverged >100 Mya and have simpler nervous systems | High — and the VO now states the sample size in plain words. The earlier phrasing ("Deep-sea octopuses, with simpler nervous systems, don't have it") generalised n=1 to a whole clade and implied a nervous-system link the paper only speculates about. |
| Nobody's proven this is why octopuses are clever | Authors' own language: "we **speculate** that high-fidelity translation may have been favored"; "could broadly support"; "**suggests** synergy". Limitations section states there are no tools to study octopus protein synthesis in vivo, and that a direct Cirrate-vs-Incirrate ribosome comparison has not been done | High — this is the paper's own position, stated in the voiceover rather than buried in a caption |

**Not claimed anywhere in this script:** that scientists found the cause of octopus intelligence; that the break affects octopus behaviour; that anything was measured in a living octopus. All fidelity results are in vitro. The nervous-system link is phylogenetic correlation across two clades.
