# BROLL — v3 Octopus RNA
**Visual mode:** text-on-motion. Footage is the bed; kinetic type is the lead performer. Eight shots, 30.0s total.

**Duration check:** 2.7 + 2.8 + 4.4 + 4.0 + 3.2 + 3.6 + 3.6 + 5.7 = **30.0s.** 72 spoken words at 150 wpm = 28.8s, plus 1.1s of scored pauses = 29.9s, plus a 0.1s final hold.

## Global continuity rules

- **Palette locked across all shots:** deep ink-blue and near-black backgrounds, one warm accent (amber-orange, pulled from octopus skin) used only on the type and on the break-site highlight. No third colour anywhere. The amber is the through-line that visually links "octopus" to "the break" to "the number".
- **Camera grammar:** shots 1–5 all move *inward* (push / dolly-in) — the video is zooming from animal to molecule. Shot 6 is the only static shot, which is what makes the statistic land. Shots 7–8 pull *back out* to animal scale, closing the loop.
- **Type system:** one condensed grotesque, all caps, heavy weight, tight tracking. Amber on dark. Type is always locked to the frame (not tracked to footage) except in shot 4, where it tracks the break point — that single exception is what makes shot 4 read as the mechanism beat.
- **Type rhythm:** words enter on the stressed syllable of the VO line, never before. Default entry is a 3-frame vertical slam with a 1-frame overshoot; default exit is a 4-frame downward wipe masked by the cut. Deviations are noted per shot.
- **Grain and depth:** consistent fine grain and a shallow vignette on every shot, including the abstract molecular ones — this is what keeps the CG shots feeling like they belong in the same film as the live footage.
- **Audio-visual sync:** every cut lands on a beat; the only mid-line cut is at 17.1s.
- **Shot lengths are derived from the VO lines, not from a uniform grid.** See the runtime check in `SCRIPT.md`. Evening them out will overrun a line — most dangerously the closing one.

## Shot 1 — 0.0–2.7s (2.7s)
**VO:** "Every octopus has a broken ribosome."

**Footage prompt:** Slow dolly-in on a single octopus resting in dark water, low-key side lighting, arms slowly unspooling and re-curling. Deep blue-black background falling off to pure black at the edges. Skin texture visible, subtle chromatophore shimmer. Shallow depth of field, eye in focus. Cinematic, documentary-grade, no visible tank or hands. 4K, 24fps, slight underwater particulate drift.

**Type treatment:** `BROKEN ON PURPOSE` enters at 0.4s as three separate word-slams, 4 frames apart, stacked left-aligned in lower third. Amber, heavy caps. Holds. At 2.6s a hairline white fracture line begins drawing across the middle of the frame — it does not complete in this shot.

---

## Shot 2 — 2.7–5.5s (2.8s)
**VO:** "The break is what makes it better."

**Footage prompt:** Continuation of the same octopus shot, dolly still pushing in, now closer — frame filled with skin and one eye. Same lighting, same particulate. Must read as an unbroken continuation of shot 1, not a new setup.

**Type treatment:** the hairline fracture from shot 1 completes across the full frame at 2.8s with a 2-frame white flash, and the two halves of the *image* offset by 12px vertically for 3 frames, then re-seat. `BROKEN ON PURPOSE` from shot 1 breaks along the same fracture line: the top half slides up and out, and `...MAKES IT BETTER` types in beneath it in amber. Deliberate visual pun — the type itself gets broken and improves.

---

## Shot 3 — 5.5–9.9s (4.4s)
**VO:** "Ribosomes build proteins. In octopuses, that strand is snapped in two."

**Footage prompt:** Hard cut to abstract macro CG: a single luminous ribbon of RNA, pale blue-white, threading through the dark interior of a large organic machine rendered as soft translucent mass. Slow travel along the ribbon, shallow focus, dust motes in the light. Bioluminescent feel, not textbook-diagram feel. At the 3-second mark of this shot the ribbon severs cleanly mid-frame, the two ends drifting a few pixels apart and holding — not exploding, not fraying. Amber light blooms from the severance point.

**Type treatment:** `SNAPPED IN TWO` — the first three words hold flat while the VO runs; on the word "snapped," the type itself splits horizontally through its own x-height and the halves separate by 6px, synced frame-exact to the ribbon severing. Locked to frame, lower third.

---

## Shot 4 — 9.9–13.9s (4.0s)
**VO:** "Right where the ribosome checks the amino acid is correct."

**Footage prompt:** Continuous push into the severed point from shot 3 — same ribbon, same lighting, no cut, camera closes the distance. As we arrive, the surrounding structure resolves: a socket-like cavity where small tagged shapes are being offered up to the strand one at a time. Amber highlight ring pulses gently around the break site. Everything else desaturates by 20% so the break site is the only warm thing on screen.

**Type treatment:** the one tracked shot. `THE PROOFREADING SEAT` is small, set on a thin amber leader line that anchors to the break site and follows it as the camera pushes, growing slightly in scale with the push. Enters with a leftward draw-on of the leader line first, then the words. Exits by retracting back along the leader into the break point at 13.7s.

---

## Shot 5 — 13.9–17.1s (3.2s)
**VO:** "In the dish, it makes fewer mistakes."

**Footage prompt:** Macro CG, same palette and grain. A chain of protein subunits assembling in a clean straight line, left to right, steady rhythm — one every few frames, each seating with a small amber flash. One incorrect subunit approaches, is refused at the socket, and drifts away out of frame darkened and desaturated. Camera drifts slowly right, following the growing chain.

**Type treatment:** `IN THE DISH — FEWER MISTAKES` sets on two lines (`IN THE DISH —` small above, `FEWER MISTAKES` heavy below); the rejected subunit passes *behind* the type as it exits — a depth cue that keeps the type inside the scene rather than pasted on top. Slowest, calmest type beat in the piece; this is the breathing room before the number.

---

## Shot 6 — 17.1–20.7s (3.6s)
**VO:** "Put that break into E. coli, and its errors halve."

**Footage prompt:** Hard cut, deliberate tonal break. Static locked-off macro of a bacterial culture — petri dish or slide under cold clinical light, pale grey-blue, rod-shaped cells drifting in shallow focus. Sterile laboratory feel, the only shot in the film with no warmth in the footage itself. No camera movement whatsoever.

**Type treatment:** the payload shot. `ERRORS` enters small and centred; then a large amber `2×` numeral scales up from 40% to 100% over 6 frames with a downward arrow, overshooting once and settling. The numeral is the largest type in the entire video — nothing else in the piece is allowed to compete with it. Everything holds dead still until the cut, matching the locked-off camera.

---

## Shot 7 — 20.7–24.3s (3.6s)
**VO:** "The one deep-sea octopus they checked didn't have it."

**Footage prompt:** Cut to genuine deep-sea footage feel: a pale, ghostly finned octopus (Grimpoteuthis / dumbo octopus type) drifting slowly above grey sediment, lit by a single hard ROV lamp from frame left, everything beyond a few metres falling to absolute black. Cold, blue-white, marine-snow particles crossing the beam. Slow pull-back begins here. Visually the exact inverse of shot 1's warmth.

**Type treatment:** `ONE DEEP-SEA OCTOPUS: NO BREAK` — set `ONE` at the same weight as the rest; the sample size is the point of the line. This is the one shot where the type is *not* amber. Set in cold white, and it enters via a slow 12-frame fade rather than a slam. The absence of the house accent colour does the work of the word "don't" before the viewer consciously reads it.

---

## Shot 8 — 24.3–30.0s (5.7s) — **the longest shot in the piece, by design**
**VO:** "Nobody's proven that's why octopuses are clever. But the break follows the brains."

**Footage prompt:** Return to the opening octopus, but wider than shot 1 — the animal now fully in frame, settled, one eye turned directly toward the lens. Warm amber key light restored. Camera completes the pull-back and comes to rest. Same water, same particulate, same grain as shot 1 so the loop reads as closed.

**Type treatment:** `NOT PROVEN.` slams in amber at 24.6s and holds. `BUT IT TRACKS.` slams beneath it at 27.6s, on the word "But", immediately after the mandated pause. Then at 29.2s the hairline fracture line from shot 2 redraws itself once across the frame and *heals* — closing to nothing — as both lines of type hold to the final frame. No exit animation; hard out on 30.0s. The healed fracture is the visual payoff of the opening "broken."
