## The one thing the viewer leaves with

The Amazon isn't untouched wilderness — a single laser survey of one small slice of it found 396 monuments nobody had ever recorded, and the researchers think more than twenty thousand are still down there.

---

## Hook

**Spoken (10 words):** "Lasers just found 396 lost monuments under the Amazon rainforest."
**On-screen (5 words):** `396 monuments. Under the Amazon.`

**Alternates considered:**
- *"Twenty thousand monuments are buried under the Amazon rainforest."* — bigger number, but 20,000 is a projection, not a count. Opening on an estimate weakens everything after it.
- *"The Amazon rainforest is growing on top of a lost civilisation."* — good image, but it's a summary, not a fact. The brief calls for a cold fact stated flat; this is a thesis stated warm.

**Why the chosen one:** 396 is a confirmed, counted, published figure. It's also an odd number — odd numbers read as real, round numbers read as marketing. Stated flat with no verb of amazement, it does the astonishing on its own.

## Caption

Lasers were flown over 4,500 km² of Amazon rainforest in Acre and Amazonas, Brazil. They found 432 earthworks — squares, circles and ditched enclosures built between 600 BC and AD 850. 396 had never been recorded. The team estimates more than 20,000 remain unfound across the region. The forest we call untouched grew on top of them.
Source: Pärssinen et al., Nature (2026).

## Fact-check

| Claim | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Survey covered ~4,500 km² of rainforest in Acre and Amazonas, Brazil | Nature (Pärssinen et al. 2026); Live Science states "nearly 1,740 sq mi (4,500 sq km)" | High |
| 432 earthworks identified; 396 previously unrecorded | Live Science and HeritageDaily both report 432 identified, 36 previously recorded → 396 new | High |
| Technology was airborne lidar / laser scanning through canopy gaps | Nature study; described in both secondary sources | High |
| Earthworks built between 600 BC and AD 850 | Live Science, quoting the study directly | High |
| Abandonment between AD 850 and 950; cause unknown | Pärssinen quoted directly in Live Science: "We do not know why the geoglyphs were suddenly abandoned" | High |
| Team estimates more than 20,000 further earthworks remain | Study title: "Over 20,000 precolonial earthworks in the Southwest Amazonia"; HeritageDaily reports an estimate range of 24,000–30,000 — **the more conservative 20,000 figure is used** | High |
| Study led from the University of Helsinki | Martti Pärssinen, University of Helsinki, is lead author | High |
| Published in Nature, July 2026 | Live Science: published Wednesday 29 July 2026 | High |
| Prior assumption of a sparsely populated, lightly modified Amazonia | Framed in both HeritageDaily and Live Science as the long-held assumption the study challenges | High |

**Deliberately excluded:** the 1.25–3 million peak population estimate and the 183,000 km² extent. Both are sourced, but both are modelled projections (population is derived from an assumed ~300 builders per geoglyph). At 40 seconds there was no room to mark them as estimates, and stating a modelled figure flat would breach the accuracy standard. Nothing in this script is unsourced.
