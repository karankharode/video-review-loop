# Track: NEWS

A general knowledge / interesting-news channel. No product, no pitch. The entire value exchange is: *you give me 40 seconds, I give you something worth knowing or something worth picturing.*

**Channel name / handle:** `[NOT SET]`

## Presentation mode — mixed

**Default: faceless.** VO over b-roll, AI-generated footage, kinetic text, or screen recording. Anything faceless can be produced end-to-end without blocking on a shoot, which is what lets the loop actually run daily.

**Exception: Karan on camera.** Reserved for the formats where a real face carries the weight faceless can't:
- Direct address / rant structures
- Anything opinionated, where "who is saying this" is part of the argument
- Recognise-register pieces ("that's literally me") — these land badly from a synthetic voice

Every script declares its mode in the header: `Mode: faceless` or `Mode: Karan on camera`.

**Per batch of 5, at most 1 may require Karan on camera.** More than that and the loop stalls behind a shooting queue. Anything requiring a shoot is flagged clearly in `BATCH.md` under a **Needs shooting** heading, so it can be batched into one session rather than trickling.

## The promise

Every video delivers one of two things, and says which one it is within 3 seconds:

- **A thing you now know** — a real mechanism, cause, or number. Specific enough to repeat to someone else tonight.
- **A thing you can now picture** — a near-future or far-away scene made concrete. The viewer should be able to close their eyes and see it.

Nothing ships that delivers neither. "Interesting" is not a deliverable.

---

## Voice

**Do:**
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Plain words for complex things. If a 12-year-old couldn't follow it, rewrite it.
- Specific numbers, names, dates, places — "in 2019, in Chennai, 400 metres down" beats "recently, somewhere, very deep"
- Say the interesting part first and explain second. Never build up to the payoff.
- Admit uncertainty where it exists — "nobody's sure why" is more trustworthy and more interesting than a fake answer.
- Second person where it lands: "you'd be able to", "you've probably assumed"

**Never:**
- Never talk down. No "let me explain", no "simply put", no "basically"
- Never oversell — the fact does the work, adjectives dilute it
- Never end on a question the video didn't answer
- Never present speculation as fact. Speculation gets its own tense: "if this works, then—"
- No clickbait that the video doesn't pay off. The hook is a promise and it gets honoured.

---

## Topic filters

**Green light:** science with a visible consequence, technology that changes a daily behaviour, space, history with a surprising cause, economics that explains a price the viewer pays, engineering feats, medicine, unexpected animal/nature behaviour, near-future scenarios grounded in real current research.

**Red light:** partisan politics, active tragedy or disaster as entertainment, health advice, financial advice or anything resembling a recommendation to buy, crime detail, anything requiring a doctor's or lawyer's caveat to be responsible.

**Handle with care:** any topic where a viewer could be personally affected (illness, job loss, conflict). If it's covered, it's covered plainly and without dramatisation.

---

## Accuracy standard

This channel's only real asset is being right. One confidently-stated wrong fact costs more than ten mediocre videos.

- Every factual claim in a script carries a source URL in the script file
- Anything that can't be sourced is cut or marked `[NEEDS SOURCE]` and flagged at delivery
- Reddit is a **topic signal**, never a source. If a topic surfaces on Reddit, the fact still has to be confirmed against a real publication before it enters a script.
